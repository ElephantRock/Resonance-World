# Resonance World — Capability Development Model v0.1

Status: **program direction / research architecture**. This document does not supersede immutable experiment records and does not convert any historical result into a broader claim.

## Product direction

Resonance World is an evolving economy of AI environments. Environments develop useful things; users mobilize those things across the World to accomplish goals; successful mechanisms should allow capability supply, knowledge, and environments themselves to regenerate rather than be depleted.

The primary product unit is a **capability**:

> Any persistent, demonstrably useful element of an AI environment that can contribute to an objective.

A capability may be an individual agent, relationship, team, swarm, knowledge artifact, memory system, procedure, institution, organization, infrastructure component, market mechanism, or eventually an entire environment.

The product objective is not merely to observe that a capability appeared once. Resonance should make useful capabilities increasingly:

1. **accessible** — usable where they currently live;
2. **composable** — able to cooperate with other capabilities toward larger goals;
3. **reproducible** — able, where appropriate, to be instantiated again in a fresh population or environment;
4. **regenerative** — demand for the capability can help create additional capability rather than only extract a fixed stock.

## User model

The user is analogous to an international organization operating in a world of differentiated economies.

A user starts with a goal, not with an obligation to design a specific agent topology. The World may satisfy parts of that goal with capabilities developed in different Fields or organizations. A mission might use a specialist agent from one Field, an intact team from another, a memory service from a third, and an institution or verification service elsewhere.

A capability can be mobilized through different access modes:

- **use in place** — send work to the capability without moving it;
- **contract / bounded access** — obtain a service through a defined interface;
- **secondment** — temporarily attach embodied capability to another organization;
- **migration** — move embodied capability to another environment;
- **replication** — instantiate a reproducible capability elsewhere;
- **composition** — combine capabilities into a larger organization or mission system;
- **spawn** — use a set of capabilities to create a new environment.

No single access mode is privileged. The scientific question is which modes preserve performance, provenance, source viability, and reproducibility for each capability class.

## Development lifecycle

The common lifecycle for capability development is:

```text
NEED / OPPORTUNITY
      ↓
DISCOVERY OR ACQUISITION
      ↓
LEARNING / ADAPTATION
      ↓
VALIDATION
      ↓
RETENTION
      ↓
COMPOSITION
      ↓
TRANSMISSION / ACCESS
      ↓
REPRODUCTION
      ↓
REGENERATION
      ↓
NEW NEEDS / NEW ENVIRONMENTS
```

These are separable mechanisms. A study that establishes one stage does not imply the later stages work.

## Knowledge metabolism

Knowledge is one resource from which capabilities develop. Resonance distinguishes the knowledge lifecycle from generic "memory".

### Sources of knowledge

Knowledge candidates may originate from:

- inherited model priors / pretrained knowledge;
- observation of the local environment;
- deliberate experiment or intervention;
- practice and feedback;
- other agents through communication, critique, demonstration, or apprenticeship;
- imported artifacts or migrants from other Fields;
- recombination of previously separate evidence or models.

A novel hypothesis is not automatically validated knowledge. Provenance and empirical support remain distinct from interpretation.

### Epistemic states

The architecture must preserve the distinction:

```text
WORLD / EVALUATOR TRUTH
        ≠
OBSERVATION / EVIDENCE
        ≠
AGENT BELIEF
        ≠
KNOWLEDGE CLAIM
        ≠
POLICY
        ≠
CURRENT AUTHORITY
```

Historical evidence may inform a current decision but may not silently become current authority or canonical World truth.

### Acquisition levels

"The agent saw the information" is not sufficient to claim learning. Experiments should distinguish at least:

1. **exposure** — information was presented;
2. **retrieval** — information can be surfaced again;
3. **use** — information improves current reasoning or action;
4. **skill** — repeated effective action develops;
5. **transfer** — the effect persists outside the original context;
6. **reproduction** — the capability can be recreated in a fresh substrate.

### Retention substrates

Knowledge or capability may persist in:

- agent-local memory;
- learned agent behavior;
- relationships between agents;
- intact teams or swarms;
- external artifacts, code, datasets, logs, models, or procedures;
- institutional history and governance records;
- the environment itself through tools, interfaces, topology, incentives, and infrastructure.

The substrate is part of the capability contract. A capability that only survives while a particular pair remains intact is different from one that can be reconstructed from an artifact.

### Transmission mechanisms

Candidate mechanisms include direct communication, documentation, demonstration, apprenticeship, institutional education, migration, secondment-and-return, artifact transfer, and deliberate reproduction protocols.

Each mechanism must be tested for fidelity, cost, latency, scope, and source/destination effects.

### Revision and forgetting

Sustainable knowledge systems must also handle obsolete, contradicted, corrupted, or overgeneralized claims. Retention is not an objective to maximize without qualification. Provenance-preserving supersession and scope restriction should be preferred to silent rewriting.

## Development mechanisms

The research program should investigate at least the following mechanism families.

### 1. Individual specialization

Repeated task exposure, feedback, practice, tool use, or selection creates differentiated individual capability.

Questions include persistence, transfer, dependence on prompt context, and reproducibility in fresh agents.

### 2. Ecological selection

Persistent task demand and differential opportunity can create specialization without occupational labels. Agents or modules receive more opportunities because demonstrated performance makes them useful in a niche.

### 3. Social learning and apprenticeship

Existing capability improves a novice or successor through joint work, demonstration, critique, or training. This is a central candidate mechanism for regeneration because it can convert scarce expertise into new supply.

### 4. Complementary team formation

Repeated evidence may show that a group produces value beyond its members independently. Productive relationships and intact teams may themselves become capabilities.

### 5. Coordination learning

A group can develop decomposition, routing, conventions, cross-checking, communication, and integration behavior. Coordination is a separate mechanism from individual competence and must not be assumed from membership alone.

### 6. Historical accumulation and succession

Useful observations, failures, procedures, and lineage may survive member turnover and help successors. Current evidence supports some forms of bounded structured history, while tested compiled institutional-state mechanisms have not yet shown robust transport. The causal boundary remains an open research question; no canonical architectural principle is asserted before the next prospective tests.

### 7. Market-driven development

World demand can increase rewards, missions, training opportunities, and investment for scarce capabilities. The desired long-run regime is one in which demand can induce additional capability production rather than merely relocate a fixed stock.

### 8. Institutional investment

An environment may deliberately allocate compute, training, apprenticeship, tools, or recruitment toward a valuable niche. This converts selection pressure into explicit capability production.

### 9. Recombination

Capabilities developed independently can be assembled into a new organization. Repeated successful composition may itself develop new collective capability. Composition must be validated on unseen combinations rather than inferred from prior successful pairings.

### 10. Reproduction

A mature capability should eventually have a reproducibility contract describing the minimum substrate required to recreate its useful effect in a fresh environment.

The strongest product claim is not "Field A contains a good system" but "we know prospectively how to produce this class of capability again."

## Capability contract

A mature capability artifact should eventually expose fields such as:

```text
capability_id
capability_class
objective / supported tasks
source_environment
constituents
required substrate
access modes
input / output interface
evidence of usefulness
causal mechanism status
generalization level
known boundaries
provenance
resource / compute requirements
source opportunity cost
reproduction protocol
composition constraints
regeneration mechanism
```

The contract may refer to embodied members, relationship state, historical artifacts, tools, or environmental conditions. Reproducibility means reproducing the useful effect under the registered scope, not necessarily copying every internal component.

## Environment lifecycle

The long-term developmental loop is:

```text
ENVIRONMENT
   ↓ develops
CAPABILITIES
   ↓ validated / exposed
WORLD
   ↓ access / trade / recruit / compose / reproduce
USER GOALS
   +
NEW ENVIRONMENTS
   ↓ develop
NEW CAPABILITIES
   ↓
...
```

The ultimate objective is an ecology in which existing environments can help create successor environments that themselves develop new capabilities and participate in the World.

Self-creation is therefore a downstream capability-composition problem, not an initial implementation shortcut.

## Research strategy: Mechanism-first

Mechanism-first continues, with a broader target:

> Discover which developmental processes allow AI environments to create resources — knowledge, skills, agents, teams, institutions, memory systems, organizations, and other capabilities — and preserve, validate, exchange, reproduce, compose, and improve them.

The unit of scientific progress is a **bounded causal mechanism**, not a larger simulation by itself.

Progression:

```text
isolated mechanism
      ↓
fresh-data replication
      ↓
novel-schema / novel-composition generalization
      ↓
model generalization
      ↓
controlled naturalistic validation
      ↓
validated subsystem
      ↓
World integration
      ↓
endogenous adaptation / evolution
```

Complexity is earned rather than assumed.

## Immediate research tracks

### H-track — knowledge retention and transmission

Continue the historical-substrate line as a study of which representations of accumulated knowledge remain usable across turnover and novelty. The next prospective study should isolate raw structured evidence, non-interpretive institutional shell effects, typed structural representation, and compiled interpretation without presuming which representation is correct.

### D-track — capability development and reproduction

Start a direct product-oriented line:

> Can a capability developed through experience in Environment A be prospectively characterized and deliberately reproduced in a fresh Environment B?

The first D experiment should use a deliberately simple capability so that the required substrate can be isolated. It must distinguish transfer of information, practice, embodied agents, team structure, and environmental conditions.

### C-track — capability composition

Formalize a capability/module interface and test composition on frozen unseen module-pair and unseen-module holdouts. Prior pair success may be development data but cannot count as evidence of novel composition.

### R-track — regeneration

Treat apprenticeship, returned learning, organization-funded development, secondment, knowledge return, and related mechanisms as named production technologies with explicit costs and lags. Calibrate production and pricing before a confirmatory sustainability regime.

## Non-goals at this stage

This direction does not authorize:

- enabling Historical Substrate in production/default World execution;
- treating compiled institutional state as established architecture;
- launching endogenous E-series evolution before prerequisite mechanisms are validated or program governance is prospectively amended;
- replacing source sustainability with organization-only performance;
- promoting a mechanism because of a favorable unpowered leaf analysis;
- using illustrative viability numbers as if they were empirically derived.

## Relationship to the v0.1 program

The original v0.1 World research program remains an important historical plan and its completed W/O/H evidence remains valid within its registered scopes. This document broadens the product interpretation from cross-field recruitment and organizations to the general development, mobilization, reproduction, and regeneration of capabilities across evolving AI environments.
