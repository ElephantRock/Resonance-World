# Resonance World — Publication Strategy

Status: **living research-program document**

Last updated: 2026-08-15

This document tracks publication opportunities as the Resonance World research program evolves. It is not itself a scientific result and does not broaden the scope of any registered claim. Immutable experiment records, preregistrations, evaluators, manifests, and mechanism-registry decisions remain authoritative for scientific claims.

## Standing publication rule

Every substantial experiment, mechanism proposal, replication, negative result, integration milestone, or literature deep dive should be evaluated on two axes in parallel:

1. **scientific value** — what bounded causal or empirical claim the work actually supports;
2. **publication value** — whether that claim strengthens an existing paper, changes a paper's structure, or warrants an independent paper.

Publication planning must not drive post-outcome retuning. A result becomes more publishable by stronger prospective controls, replication, generalization, and failure analysis—not by broadening the wording of the claim.

## Publication maturity scale

### P0 — Observation

An interesting pattern, failure, engineering behavior, or exploratory result. It can motivate a prospective experiment but is not evidence for a paper claim by itself.

### P1 — Paper-worthy result

A bounded result with credible provenance and a meaningful scientific interpretation. It belongs in a paper but may not support a paper on its own.

### P2 — Publishable contribution

A coherent contribution supported by suitable controls, ablations, replication, and scope restrictions. It can anchor a submission when combined with adequate related work, analysis, and reproducibility artifacts.

### P3 — Independent paper

A contribution with enough novelty, evidence, and conceptual identity that separating it from the main Resonance World paper improves clarity rather than fragmenting the research program.

The default should be consolidation. A new paper is justified only when its central claim, experimental logic, and intended audience are meaningfully distinct from the existing portfolio.

# Current publication portfolio

## Paper A — Institutional memory and historical accumulation under turnover

**Working title:** *Institutional Memory Across Turnover in Artificial Cognitive Societies*

**Current maturity:** strong P2 candidate; not yet submission-ready.

### Core scientific angle

Artificial societies need not lose all useful accumulated experience when their members turn over. The H-series asks which representations of bounded historical evidence remain usable by successors, how that usefulness behaves across generations, and which seemingly plausible institutional-memory mechanisms fail.

The strongest paper is not simply "memory helps." The more defensible angle is:

> Under controlled artificial-society conditions, structured historical evidence can preserve and accumulate decision-relevant information across complete member turnover, including use by stochastic successors, but the benefit is representation- and mechanism-dependent rather than a generic property of institutional memory.

This framing uses both the positive and negative H-series results and makes the causal boundary itself part of the contribution.

### Existing evidence spine

- **H1:** structured bounded history has measurable value under the registered scope.
- **H2:** useful historical information can persist under member turnover.
- **H3:** accumulation can extend across multiple generations under complete turnover.
- **H4:** a stochastic successor can use accumulated organizational history under the registered bounded setting.
- **H5:** tested institutional mediation did not survive confirmation; preserve as a negative mechanism result.
- **H6:** relevance gating produced a positive preregistered mechanism result.
- **H7:** selective routing failed fresh-unit confirmation; preserve as a negative boundary result.
- **H8:** history representation has a classified boundary; current evidence remains single-model and bounded.

### Why this is publishable

The series contains a rare combination of positive results, preregistered negative results, complete turnover, multi-generation accumulation, stochastic model use, and explicit representation/mechanism boundaries. That allows the paper to make a stronger scientific contribution than a single benchmark win: it can characterize conditions under which institutional history survives succession and conditions under which it does not.

### Evidence still needed before submission

Priority order:

1. **Model generalization.** At least one independent model family or otherwise credible model-disjoint replication of the most important positive mechanism.
2. **Task/schema generalization.** Demonstrate that the effect is not confined to one exact decision schema or fixture family.
3. **Unified effect analysis.** Report effect sizes and uncertainty consistently across H1–H8 instead of presenting only pass/fail gates.
4. **Representation ablation.** Prospectively isolate raw structured evidence, non-interpretive shell effects, typed structural representation, and compiled interpretation.
5. **Information-capacity control.** Separate representation quality from simple token/context-volume advantages.
6. **Failure analysis.** Explain H5 and H7 failures mechanistically rather than treating them as discarded leaves.
7. **Naturalistic stress test.** One controlled but less synthetic setting would materially improve external validity.

### Proposed paper structure

1. **Introduction**
   - succession is a central failure mode for persistent AI institutions;
   - distinguish individual memory from institutional history;
   - state the bounded claim and nonclaims.
2. **Related work**
   - LLM/agent memory;
   - organizational learning and institutional memory;
   - multi-agent succession and cultural transmission;
   - retrieval, summarization, and structured-history representations.
3. **Experimental system and research governance**
   - Resonance World environment;
   - turnover protocol;
   - prospective registration, frozen evaluators, provenance, and replication boundaries.
4. **H1–H4: Establishing historical persistence**
   - bounded-history value;
   - member turnover;
   - multi-generation accumulation;
   - stochastic successor use.
5. **H5–H8: Locating the mechanism boundary**
   - institutional mediation failure;
   - relevance-gating result;
   - selective-routing non-replication;
   - representation boundary.
6. **Cross-series analysis**
   - unified effect sizes;
   - information budget;
   - robustness and sensitivity;
   - causal interpretation.
7. **Failure modes and scope restrictions**
8. **Discussion**
   - implications for persistent AI organizations;
   - history as evidence versus history as authority;
   - implications for capability development and succession.
9. **Limitations and conclusion**

This should remain distinct from the capability-reproduction paper unless later D-series evidence shows that historical accumulation is itself sufficient to reproduce a capability in a fresh environment. At present H establishes retention/use across succession, not general capability reproduction.

## Paper B — Prospective capability reproduction

**Working title:** *Reproducing Developed Capabilities in Fresh AI Populations*

**Current maturity:** P1 moving toward P2. Potential flagship paper if D2 and later generalization succeed.

### Core scientific angle

The D-track asks a stronger question than transfer or retrieval:

> Can a capability that developed through experience in one AI environment be prospectively characterized by its required substrate and deliberately recreated in a fresh population or environment?

The central conceptual contribution is the distinction between **capability use**, **capability transfer**, and **capability reproduction**. Reproduction requires creating the useful effect again in a fresh substrate, not merely moving the original agent or exposing a new agent to descriptive information.

### Existing evidence spine

- **D1:** controlled deterministic individual-specialist capability-reproduction discovery result.
- **D1b:** fresh identity- and seed-disjoint replication of the unchanged D1 mechanism.
- **Independent acceptance:** D1/D1b promoted to internally replicated within the controlled deterministic individual-specialist substrate.
- **D2:** stochastic model-mediated reproduction program scaffolded prospectively; initial development calibration failed to establish learnability, followed by a revised development-only calibration preserving source learnability. D2 proper remains bounded by prospective authorization and must not inherit a positive conclusion from D1.

### Strongest eventual claim

If D2 and subsequent holdouts succeed, the paper should target:

> A developed capability can be prospectively decomposed into a reproducibility contract whose intervention recreates the useful effect in fresh agents, and this effect survives fresh cohorts, stochastic model execution, and at least one novel-schema or model-generalization test.

Until those conditions are met, the paper must stay scoped to deterministic individual-specialist reproduction.

### Evidence still needed before submission

1. Successful **stochastic model-mediated** reproduction under D2 or a prospectively amended successor experiment.
2. At least one **model-disjoint** replication.
3. A **novel-schema** or novel-task-family holdout.
4. Explicit ablations over substrate components: information, practice, agent embodiment, team structure, environment structure.
5. A falsifiable **minimum sufficient substrate** analysis.
6. Cost/latency accounting for reproduction versus direct use, migration, or retraining.
7. At least one capability class beyond the simplest individual-specialist mechanism, if feasible without diluting causal identification.

### Proposed paper structure

1. **Introduction: from transfer to reproduction**
2. **Capability-development framework**
   - capability definition;
   - access modes;
   - reproduction versus retrieval/transfer/migration;
   - reproducibility contract.
3. **Experimental methodology**
   - source capability development;
   - fresh substrate creation;
   - prospective intervention;
   - acceptance metrics and controls.
4. **D1 discovery**
5. **D1b fresh-cohort replication**
6. **D2 stochastic model-mediated reproduction**
7. **Substrate ablations**
8. **Generalization tests**
9. **Cost, failure, and boundary analysis**
10. **Discussion and implications for regenerative AI environments**

### Key figures/tables

- **Figure 1:** capability lifecycle and the precise location of reproduction.
- **Figure 2:** source-development → artifact/contract → fresh-population reproduction pipeline.
- **Figure 3:** D1 and D1b replication effects.
- **Figure 4:** stochastic D2 reproduction versus source-learnability and no-reproduction controls.
- **Figure 5:** minimum-sufficient-substrate ablation graph.
- **Table 1:** claim ladder from deterministic reproduction to naturalistic/model-generalized reproduction.

### Publication decision rule

Do **not** submit this as a broad capability-reproduction paper while the evidence is only deterministic and individual-specialist. The value of the concept depends on demonstrating that reproduction survives at least one meaningful stochastic and generalization boundary.

## Paper C — Non-interfering longitudinal observability for artificial societies

**Working title:** *ContextGraph Observatory: Non-Interfering Longitudinal Instrumentation for Artificial Multi-Agent Systems*

**Current maturity:** P2 methods/systems candidate, but ownership should be coordinated with the independently released ContextGraph project.

### Core scientific angle

Longitudinal artificial-society experiments require rich evidence about interactions and state transitions, but instrumentation can itself change behavior or leak privileged evaluator state. The O-series establishes an observer-side contract for collecting and reconstructing longitudinal evidence without changing participant outcomes under the registered scope.

### Existing evidence spine

- ContextGraph v0.1.0 adopted as an observer-only World substrate.
- **O0:** passive Observatory non-interference established under the registered World units.
- **O1:** observer-side reconstruction validity established under hidden-state exclusion and frozen benchmark planes.
- **O2:** longitudinal scientific utility established under the amended registered contract.

### Evidence still needed

1. Multi-environment generalization beyond Resonance World.
2. Overhead/scale benchmarks.
3. Comparison with conventional logging/event-sourcing/tracing alternatives.
4. Clear threat model for hidden-state leakage and instrumentation-induced treatment changes.
5. Examples showing that the observatory enables analyses that would otherwise be unreliable or unrecoverable.

### Proposed paper structure

1. Observability problem in autonomous multi-agent experiments.
2. Design requirements: non-interference, provenance, reconstruction, stopping completeness, hidden-state isolation.
3. ContextGraph data/runtime architecture.
4. O0 non-interference study.
5. O1 reconstruction-validity study.
6. O2 longitudinal-utility study.
7. Scaling and overhead.
8. Cross-environment case studies.
9. Limitations and threat model.

### Portfolio note

If ContextGraph has its own publication program, this paper should live there, with Resonance World as a principal validation environment. Resonance World should cite the method rather than duplicating ownership.

## Paper D — Capability economies and regenerative artificial environments

**Working title:** *From Capability Markets to Regenerative AI Environments*

**Current maturity:** P0/P1 conceptual program; not an empirical paper yet.

### Core angle

The long-run Resonance World hypothesis is not simply that agents can be recruited across societies. It is that differentiated AI environments can develop persistent capabilities that become accessible, composable, reproducible, and eventually regenerative through demand, institutions, learning, and reproduction.

A future paper becomes scientifically compelling when the program can show an endogenous loop such as:

> demand for a scarce capability changes incentives or investment, which prospectively causes additional capability production, and the resulting supply satisfies later demand without irreversibly degrading source environments.

### Required evidence

- validated capability classes;
- explicit production functions with compute/time costs;
- market or institutional interventions that change capability supply;
- source-environment sustainability metrics;
- causal distinction between relocation and production;
- unseen-demand or shock holdout;
- at least one regenerative mechanism such as apprenticeship, secondment-and-return, institution-funded development, or deliberate reproduction.

### Proposed eventual structure

1. Problem: extraction versus regeneration in AI capability markets.
2. World model and capability economy.
3. Production mechanisms and cost model.
4. Market/institutional treatments.
5. Supply response and source sustainability.
6. Shock/generalization experiments.
7. Welfare, concentration, and ecological tradeoffs.
8. Discussion: artificial economies as capability-development systems.

This paper should not be accelerated until the C/R tracks establish real production mechanisms.

## Paper E — Cross-society organization and world-level composition

**Working title:** *Composing Organizations from Independently Developed Artificial Societies*

**Current maturity:** P1 program target; current W-series evidence is mixed/negative and does not yet support the original broad v0.1 hypothesis.

### Core angle

The original World question remains scientifically important:

> Can capabilities developed independently in different artificial societies be composed into organizations that solve unseen problems better than equally resourced purpose-built baselines without collapsing the productive diversity of the source societies?

### Current interpretation

The completed W-series and W9 campaign should be treated as mechanism-development evidence, including negative and mixed results. Failure to validate candidate coalition or integrated-market mechanisms is informative for designing the next prospective composition program, but it should not be narrated as support for the original organizational-advantage claim.

### Required evidence before this becomes a flagship empirical paper

- prospectively validated capability-composition interface;
- unseen module-pair and unseen-module holdouts;
- matched purpose-built and random-composition baselines;
- equal model/tool/compute controls;
- source-environment sustainability metrics;
- fresh-cohort replication;
- ideally more than one mission family.

### Proposed eventual structure

1. Independent artificial societies and the composition problem.
2. Portable capability contracts.
3. Organization construction and control baselines.
4. Unseen-composition experiments.
5. Organizational performance.
6. Source-field ecological effects.
7. Failure modes and concentration effects.
8. Generalization to new missions and source combinations.

# Supporting publication opportunities

## Negative-results / mechanism-boundary paper

Do not create a standalone negative-results paper merely because several mechanisms failed. However, if H5/H7, W9, and future prospectively registered failures reveal a coherent structural pattern—for example, that interpreted/compiled summaries systematically fail where raw or typed evidence survives—then a dedicated mechanism-boundary paper may become justified.

Decision threshold: the negative results must support a positive explanatory claim about **why** a class of mechanisms fails, not simply catalogue non-significant experiments.

## Research-governance / computational-science methods paper

Resonance World already uses unusually strong mechanisms for prospective freezing, exact-head execution, immutable manifests, fresh-cohort replication, non-self-promoting mechanism registries, and explicit claim/nonclaim boundaries.

This may eventually support a methods or position paper on reproducible experimental science with model-mediated autonomous systems. It should remain supporting material unless the program can demonstrate that these controls detect or prevent concrete failure modes that ordinary agent-evaluation practice misses.

# Paper-triage checklist for every new result

For each completed experiment or substantial analysis, record:

1. **Result:** what happened, including negative or null outcomes.
2. **Bounded claim:** the strongest statement directly supported.
3. **Nonclaims:** tempting broader statements that remain unsupported.
4. **Novelty:** what this adds beyond both prior literature and prior Resonance results.
5. **Paper destination:** Paper A/B/C/D/E, supporting appendix, or new candidate.
6. **Maturity:** P0/P1/P2/P3.
7. **Missing evidence:** replication, ablation, generalization, statistics, or naturalistic validation.
8. **Figure/table candidate:** whether the result changes the planned visual evidence.
9. **Structure impact:** whether a paper section or thesis should change.
10. **Next experiment:** the highest-value prospective test for publication-grade evidence.

# Immediate publication priorities

## Priority 1 — Finish the H-series causal boundary

The H-track is currently closest to a coherent paper. The next prospective work should prioritize representation isolation, model generalization, and unified effect analysis rather than adding more loosely related history variants.

## Priority 2 — Protect the D-series flagship claim

Do not overstate D1/D1b. The publication value of the D-track depends on crossing the stochastic/model-mediated and generalization boundary while preserving prospective controls. A failed D2 is scientifically useful because it identifies which parts of reproduction do not transport.

## Priority 3 — Keep ContextGraph publication ownership clean

Treat the Observatory evidence as a methods contribution and coordinate it with the separate ContextGraph repository rather than duplicating a systems paper in World.

## Priority 4 — Delay broad World/ecology claims

Do not force W-series mixed results into the original v0.1 flagship hypothesis. The stronger route is to validate capability reproduction, composition, and regeneration first, then return to cross-society organizations with mechanisms that have independently earned integration.

# Venue-fit rubric

Do not select a venue before the paper's contribution class is stable. Evaluate venues by audience rather than prestige alone:

- **multi-agent systems / autonomous agents:** organization, coordination, institutions, inter-agent learning;
- **machine learning:** capability learning/reproduction, generalization, empirical methodology;
- **AI systems:** observability, reproducibility infrastructure, longitudinal experimentation;
- **artificial life / complex systems:** emergence, turnover, cultural/institutional persistence, evolving capability economies;
- **AI safety / governance workshops:** provenance, institutional memory, authority boundaries, persistent autonomous organizations.

Current venue deadlines and scope should be re-checked when a candidate reaches P2.

# Publication integrity rules

1. A paper claim must never exceed the exact scope of the evidence used to support it.
2. Negative confirmatory results remain first-class evidence and should not disappear from the narrative.
3. Development data and confirmatory data must remain distinguishable in the manuscript.
4. Post-outcome amendments must be visible and justified.
5. Model/provider generalization must not be implied from a single-model result.
6. Deterministic mechanism validation must not be described as naturalistic model learning.
7. Historical retention must not be described as reproduction unless a fresh substrate recreates the useful capability.
8. Recruitment, migration, transfer, use-in-place, and reproduction are distinct interventions.
9. A successful organization does not establish a healthy World if source environments are depleted.
10. Publication strategy may prioritize prospective tests but may not alter registered outcomes or retrofit hypotheses.

This document should evolve whenever a result materially changes the scientific thesis, paper boundary, evidence requirements, or submission readiness of the Resonance World research portfolio.
