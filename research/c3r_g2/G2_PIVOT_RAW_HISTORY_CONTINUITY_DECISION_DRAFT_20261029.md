## Why this pivot (evidence-grounded)

The full-repository evidence map (deliverable A) identified the
program's strongest confirmatory thread:

- **H1–H4:** structured accumulating RAW history causally improves
  decisions of fixed controllers AND fresh stochastic successors —
  through complete member replacement and five generations, on a LIVE
  model, Holm-corrected (H4: 35/36 vs 19/36, p≈7.6e-6).
- Every layer built OVER raw history failed to add value (H5 negative;
  H8 no aggregate improvement; H6/H7 contradictory), and the
  organizational-memory representations of W5A/Piano-5-series never
  passed their materiality gates (+3pp; four near-misses with a
  documented memory-FORMATION-QUALITY bottleneck).
- The now-closed G2 line confirmed the same boundary from the
  compilation side: representation granularity trades detail for
  reuse, and its net value over competent raw-evidence use is NOT
  demonstrable at confirmatory power within a 2,500-send envelope.

The unanswered architectural question that the evidence points to:
**does the raw-history channel itself carry INSTITUTIONAL properties —
i.e., does an organization that preserves and grows an admitted,
graded, structured history keep its capability through repeated
membership turnover, at what cost, and under which curation rules?**

## Candidate discriminating question (for registration, not yet registered)

> Under a fixed model and matched information budgets, does an
> organization whose only persistent asset is an accumulating
> evaluator-graded structured history sustain task performance across
> successive complete member replacements — and what formation/curation
> rules (admission thresholds, history growth, retrieval budgets)
> govern the decay or persistence of that capability?

Why it is NOT a re-run of H1–H4: those established single-decision
improvement and (H3/H4) accumulation with replacement in a BENCH
harness. The institutional question adds: (a) repeated generational
turnover as the experimental unit's timeline; (b) organizational
cost/curriculum accounting (history size, admission cost, retrieval
budget); (c) the FAILURE modes of the channel (when does accumulated
history start to hurt — stale evidence, adversarial records, retrieval
saturation); (d) the comparison against a RESET organization at every
generation under identical member quality and budgets.

## Proposed arm skeleton (draft; powers only after owner approval)

| arm | persistent asset | tests |
|---|---|---|
| CONTINUITY | admitted graded structured history, growing under frozen curation rules | institutional persistence |
| RESET | same organization reset each generation, same members' quality/budget | pure-membership control |
| FROZEN-SNAPSHOT | history frozen at generation 0 | accumulation vs retention |

Primary estimand: performance gap CONTINUITY − RESET across
generations (does the organization outlive its members?); secondary:
CONTINUITY − FROZEN-SNAPSHOT (does continued accumulation matter?);
failure-mode analyses under stressed curation.
